The big story
The Binnenhof, Den Haag. Via Wikimedia Commons.

The government decides the government itself is too big

On Monday the 26th of October, State Secretary Eric van der Burg told the country that roughly sixteen percent of the national civil service, about twenty five thousand jobs, will disappear by 2035. The plan, built around automating routine paperwork and letting artificial intelligence do more of the work a desk once did, is meant to save about three and a half billion euros a year once it is fully in place. Police and defense are excluded. Prime Minister Rob Jetten's cabinet, a minority government since February, is framing it as modernizing rather than cutting corners, though nobody loses a job painlessly, even over a decade.

Nothing about this touches the family directly, but it is worth knowing before you arrive: the Dutch civil service is the body that will eventually stamp your residence permits, your housing registration, and Amellia's future school enrollment, so a decade of it quietly reorganizing itself is background noise worth having heard once, rather than being surprised by later.

🌷 Tulp's lesson: de ambtenaar, the civil servant, the person behind every one of those stamps and forms, said AHMT-uh-nahr, with the accent up front.

A few quick things, told quickly
The Rijksmuseum, Amsterdam. Via Wikimedia Commons.

The boy who left Rotterdam steerage class comes home a legend

In 1926, an unknown twenty two year old named Willem de Kooning stowed away on a freighter out of Rotterdam bound for America. A hundred years later, almost to the month, the Rijksmuseum opened "Willem de Kooning at Work," the biggest exhibition of his work Europe has seen in forty years, built with Chicago's Art Institute around roughly a hundred and twenty drawings, paintings, and sculptures. Two of his most famous canvases now hang permanently in the museum's own Gallery of Honour alongside the Rembrandts and Vermeers.

A monthlong diplomatic chill with Israel shows no sign of thawing

Back in late September, Israel stripped the diplomatic status of Dutch officials working in Ramallah, after the Netherlands banned imports of goods made in Israeli settlements in the occupied West Bank. A month later, the Dutch government is still, in its own foreign minister's words, "considering its response," and nothing has moved. France and Canada passed similar bans around the same time without facing any retaliation at all.

Storm Austen is the first named storm of the Dutch autumn

Forecasters issued weather warnings this week as Storm Austen approached, with the heaviest gusts expected along the southwest coast, which includes the stretch Scheveningen sits on. A second system was already queued up behind it. Nothing dramatic is guaranteed, but it is the kind of week to keep the bicycle lights charged and the good coat within reach.

Dish of the week
Broodje kroket. Photo via Wikimedia Commons.

The one snack that breaks the Dutch rule of just enough

A broodje kroket is a deep fried log of thick, well seasoned beef ragout, breaded and golden on the outside, pressed into a white roll with a thin layer of mustard. It is sold hot from the automat wall or the counter of nearly every snackbar and railway kiosk in the country, and it is, by Dutch standards, a genuinely indulgent thing to eat standing up.

That matters because the Dutch are otherwise famous for exactly the opposite instinct. Offer a Dutch host one biscuit with your afternoon coffee and they will consider that a perfectly generous koffietijd, no seconds implied or expected. The kroket is the one place that same culture lets itself go a little, no rationing, no one-biscuit rule, just a hot roll eaten fast before the next train.

🌷 Tulp's lesson: koffietijd, literally coffee time, the brief, ritualized mid-morning pause nearly every Dutch workplace and household still keeps.

🌷 Tulp's Dutch Calendar Radar

The whole country just lost an hour of evening

The clocks went back an hour in the night of Saturday the 24th to Sunday the 25th, so it is now proper dark here well before six in the evening, same as every October since the EU rule came in. Everyone's phone adjusted itself; everyone's oven clock did not. The lantern walks and the steamboat I told you about last issue are now only two weeks out, so keep that date circled rather than me explaining it twice.

Artwork of the week
Gerard ter Borch, Gallant Conversation, known as "The Paternal Admonition," about 1654. Rijksmuseum, via Wikimedia Commons.

The painting that fooled two centuries of its own viewers

For generations this scene was read exactly as its nickname suggests: a stern father scolding his daughter while an officer looks on, a tidy little morality lesson. Ter Borch's own contemporaries almost certainly saw it very differently, as a young woman being quietly propositioned, with the man offering her coins and her own reaction kept hidden because her back is turned to us.

The genius is in exactly that withheld face. Ter Borch gives you every fold of her satin dress, rendered so precisely you can hear it, and not one clue about what she is actually feeling. A nearly identical second version hangs in Berlin, reunited with this one for the first time in over three hundred fifty years only a few seasons ago.

The Hague & Scheveningen dispatch
The Kurhaus, Scheveningen boulevard. Via Wikimedia Commons.

Your new neighborhood used to be a painter's whole universe

The Kurhaus went up in 1884 and 1885, just a few years after a local painter named Hendrik Willem Mesdag finished something far stranger just down the boulevard: a cylindrical painting of this same beach and these same dunes, more than fourteen metres tall and forty metres around, still on view today exactly where he built it, one of the oldest surviving panorama paintings anywhere in the world.

Museum Panorama Mesdag is marking the occasion properly. Since the 3rd of October and running through next February, it is hosting the first major retrospective of Betzy Akersloot-Berg, the Norwegian-Dutch painter who was Mesdag's student and close friend, with more than sixty works on loan from Norway, Sweden, and the Netherlands. A quiet, serious show, five minutes from where Alec and Irena already walk the dog. More on the Akersloot-Berg exhibition →

🌷 a gift from Tulp
schemer
say it: SKHAY-mer, that first sound a soft scrape in the throat
Twilight, the long, dim stretch between daylight and real dark that a Dutch autumn evening sits inside for what feels like an hour at a time. It is also used for anything half clear, a hazy memory, an argument still half formed, borrowing the same in-between feeling.
A couple of ways you will hear it used:
"Het wordt al vroeg schemer."  (It is already getting dim and dusky early.)
"In de schemering lijkt alles anders."  (Everything looks different at twilight.)
