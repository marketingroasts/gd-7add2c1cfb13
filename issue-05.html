31weeks to go
We are counting down to 15 March 2027, little Amellia's third birthday, the day you all arrive for good. Thirty one weeks. Somewhere between now and then the leaves will turn, the ice will come, and one whole Dutch Christmas will pass, and I intend to walk you through every bit of it.

A letter from Tulp de Gid 🌷

Lieve Geltovas, what a week to be looking up at the sky. Tomorrow evening the moon is going to take an enormous bite out of the sun right over your future home, nearly the whole disc of it, and I could not let that pass without writing to you first. There is football again too, the whole country back in its chair in front of the television, a little bus that drives itself has started carrying people around a theme park down in Brabant, and the schoolchildren up north are about to trade their buckets and spades for backpacks. Down at the coast the volleyball nets are already going up on the sand beside the old pier. Come and sit with me, my dears, there is a great deal to get through.

The big story
Crescent shadows cast by a partial eclipse filtering through leaves, the safest way to watch one. Photo via Wikimedia Commons.

An almost total eclipse, right over your heads

Tomorrow evening, Wednesday, the moon is going to slide in front of the sun and the whole of the Netherlands gets a front row seat. It begins quietly around 19:14, and by the time it peaks around 20:11 the moon will have covered close to nine tenths of the sun's disc, closer to ninety percent down toward Bergen op Zoom, a little less the further north you are. It is not a full eclipse, the true darkness stays far off over Greenland, Iceland, and northern Spain, but the Netherlands sits so close to that path that the sky here will still do something properly strange: not dark exactly, but a flat, held-breath sort of dim, with the sun hanging low and orange in the northwest as it happens. The forecast is clear skies, so the whole country should get a clean look.

Whatever you do, none of you are to stare straight at it, not even Amellia, not even for a second, not without proper eclipse glasses. Here is the trick I love best and the one I would do with a two year old: stand under any leafy tree and look down at the ground instead. Every little gap between the leaves turns into its own tiny pinhole camera, and the whole pavement fills with hundreds of miniature crescent suns, shifting and narrowing as the real one does overhead. It is safe, it is free, and it is far more magical than looking up.

🌷 Tulp's lesson: a solar eclipse is a zonsverduistering, literally a sun-darkening. It is one of those lovely Dutch words that simply describes exactly what it is, no fuss, no borrowed Latin. You will find the Dutch do that with a great many words once you start listening for it.

The whole country is back in its seat

Football is properly back. Ajax and Feyenoord both won their opening matches of the new season under brand new managers, Ajax coming from behind away at PEC Zwolle and Feyenoord's new captain scoring the only goal at Sparta Rotterdam, while PSV had to settle for a late, late draw at Fortuna Sittard. It is a small thing, but it means every cafe and living room in the country has its Saturday shape back. I rather suspect Alec already has an opinion about how this season is going to go.

One more scorcher before the sky dims

The weather people have called a fifth heatwave of the summer, with the mercury climbing to about 35 degrees in the southeast on Thursday and heat warnings likely for Thursday and Friday. It is a strange bit of timing, one blazing hot day, then tomorrow evening the sky quietly dimming for the eclipse, then heat again on its heels. If anyone is out and about this week, water, shade, and a hat are not optional.

A bus that drives itself to the fairy tale forest

Down at the Efteling, the great fairy tale theme park in Brabant, a small driverless shuttle bus has started carrying guests between the regular bus stop and the park's hotels, the second such bus now approved for Dutch public roads. A safety driver still rides along and takes over on the trickier stretches, but the easy part of the route runs itself. It is a tiny preview of the sort of quiet, everyday automation the Netherlands keeps testing out, and a nice little sign of the kind of place you are all moving to.

My little pick
A Pathe cinema, Amsterdam. Photo via Wikimedia Commons.

A film three thousand years in the making fills the Dutch cinemas

Christopher Nolan's new film, a telling of Homer's Odyssey, has just passed one million tickets sold in the Netherlands alone, only about three and a half weeks after it opened here. That is a genuinely enormous number for this country and this sort of film, a long, serious, three hour drama rather than a superhero picture. It has been quietly filling Dutch cinemas all August, right alongside the heatwaves and the football.

I mention it because it might be the easiest, most air conditioned way to spend one of these hot afternoons this week, and because there is something rather nice about the whole country, on the eve of a rare eclipse, sitting together in the dark watching a very old story about a long journey home. Rather fitting for this family, I think.

Dish of the week
Photo via Wikimedia Commons.

Kibbeling, the little bites from the sea

This week's dish comes straight off the sand, since half the country is heading for the beach one way or another. Kibbeling is small chunks of white fish, traditionally cod, dipped in a light, well spiced batter and fried until the outside is deeply crisp and the inside stays soft and flaky. You will find it sold by weight in a paper tray at nearly any fish stall, market, or seaside town, Scheveningen very much included, always with a little plastic fork and a tub of garlicky sauce for dunking.

It began as a practical, waste nothing dish, the smaller offcuts of fish that were not fit to sell whole, and it turned into one of the most beloved snacks in the country. Eat it standing up, straight out of the tray, ideally somewhere with a sea view. Amellia will very likely want to steal chips off whoever is holding the tray, which is exactly the correct way to eat it as a family.

🌷 Tulp's lesson: if you find yourself at a stall, the phrase you want is "Mag ik een bakje kibbeling?", which means "May I have a portion of kibbeling?" Say it with confidence and you will be treated like a local within your first week.

Artwork of the week
Johannes Vermeer, The Milkmaid (about 1660), Rijksmuseum, Amsterdam. Public domain, via Wikimedia Commons.

The woman who has been quietly pouring milk for four hundred years

Vermeer painted a great many quiet rooms, but none quite so beloved as this one. A kitchen maid stands at a table by a window, entirely absorbed in the small, ordinary task of pouring milk from a jug into a bowl, and Vermeer paints the whole scene as though the light itself were the real subject. Look closely at the loaves of bread on the table. He rendered the crust with little dabs of thick, raised paint that catch real light as you walk past, so the bread looks almost good enough to reach for, three hundred and sixty years on.

There is nothing dramatic happening here at all, and that is rather the point. It is one plain woman, one plain task, painted with more care and patience than most artists give a coronation. This one hangs in the Rijksmuseum in Amsterdam, and once you are settled it is well worth the short train ride up from Den Haag Centraal just to stand in front of her.

🌷 Tulp's Dutch Calendar Radar

Three regions, three different first days of school

No feast day on the horizon just yet, but a very Dutch bit of scheduling is coming up. The country is split into three regions for the summer holiday, north, central, and south, and each one goes back to school on its own staggered date so that everyone is not fighting for the same motorway home at the same time. The north goes back first, on Monday the 17th of August, just six days from now, the south follows on Monday the 24th, and the central region, which includes the Hague, does not return until after the 30th. It is the sort of quietly sensible planning you will meet again and again here.

One day, not too many years from now, this same little calendar will matter to your own family, when Amellia has her own first day to get ready for.

The Hague & Scheveningen dispatch
The Scheveningen Pier. Photo via Wikimedia Commons.

The old pier gets a sporty new neighbour

A little history first, since you will be living in its shadow. The very first Scheveningen pier opened in May 1901, right in front of the grand old Kurhaus, a wooden walkway on steel legs that the whole town was proud of. The Germans tore its middle section out during the war to make an invasion harder, and the rest burned in 1943. What stands there now is the concrete pier rebuilt in 1961, the one you already know, and it has been quietly reinventing itself ever since.

This week the beach right beside it turns into a stadium of its own. The Summer BeachLife Volleyball tournament runs the two weekends from Friday the 14th through Sunday the 23rd of August, courts dug straight into the sand within sight of the pier, players diving for the ball with the sea right behind them. Free to wander down and watch, and about as Scheveningen an afternoon as you could ask for. More about BeachLife Volleyball →

🌷 a gift from Tulp
fiets
say it: like "feets", a short, clipped ee
Your bicycle, and quite possibly the single most useful word you will ever learn here. This is not a country that waits for a bus if it can help it. Most train stations, Den Haag Centraal very much included, keep a vast fietsenstalling, a bicycle parking hall, holding thousands of bikes at once, and many also rent out sturdy blue OV-fiets bikes by the hour for exactly the kind of last stretch a bus route cannot quite reach. Learn to ride comfortably in traffic and an enormous amount of daily life here opens up without ever needing a car.
A few ways you will hear it used, so it settles in the ear:
"Ik ga met de fiets."  (I am going by bike.)
"Waar kan ik een fiets huren?"  (Where can I rent a bike?)
"Pas op voor de fietsers!"  (Watch out for the cyclists!)
"Zullen we naar het strand fietsen?"  (Shall we cycle to the beach?)

🌷 Tulp's question for the table

Once you can walk, cycle, or take a train nearly everywhere, what is the first everyday errand you picture doing completely differently than you do today.

That is the letter, my dears. A darkened sky, a stadium full of noise again, a little bus that needs no driver, fish from a paper tray, and a woman who has been quietly pouring milk since long before any of us were born. Different sorts of wonder, all in one ordinary week, which is rather how I would describe this whole country to you if you asked me to do it in one sentence.

Watch the sky tomorrow evening if you can, all of you, wherever you are standing. And enjoy your call this week.

Hou je haaks, which is our way of saying take good care of yourself, hold yourself steady. Liefs, Tulp.
